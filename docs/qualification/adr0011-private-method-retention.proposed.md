# ADR 0011 private D/R transaction retention — 24-hour replay window

**Status: OPTIONAL DIAGNOSTIC; SELECTED REPLAY WINDOW AND LAZY CLEANUP; POLICY/IMPLEMENTATION UNFROZEN.** The user selected a 24-hour private, default-off exact-frame replay window with lazy physical cleanup **if exact-frame diagnostics are used**. This window is not a prerequisite for a canonical D/R query/result transaction, occurrence admission, or Leiden. This is not authorization to retain production frames yet, a selected numeric policy or schema, an issued method receipt, a terminal-member verdict, an admitted occurrence, or public enablement. [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-versioned-definition-reference-grouping-evidence.md) selects the CALLS-parity `NO_PRODUCER_AUTHENTICATION` ceiling. A same-process actor may fabricate a self-consistent record; retention and digest checks do not prevent that.

## Optional frame-diagnostic policy for qualification

- **Scope:** exact framed JSON-RPC request and *selected keyed* success response, only for `textDocument/definition` or `textDocument/references` after the managed writer completes, the owned reader accepts the exact session/generation/key, selected method result is successful, and local frame correspondence passes. An unmatched notification, server error, timeout, stale generation, partial write, malformed result, or over-cap frame cannot produce a successful **frame-diagnostic** record; it does not by itself decide the separate canonical query/result transaction. Failed transactions need a separately specified redacted terminal accounting path, never fabricated success/empty evidence.
- **Default:** absent an explicit manager-owned private policy and pinned private publication root, retain nothing. Method-transport callers and public CLI/MCP requests cannot select a root, publication selector, or raw frame capture. Do not reuse Graph V5 source-capture publication as a method receipt.
- **Bounds to freeze:** candidate per-frame ceiling is 2 MiB for request and 2 MiB for selected response, with a combined maximum of 4 MiB per success, plus independent transaction-count, total-retained-bytes, live-wire, message, request, work, and time bounds. These are **proposed**, not qualified values; an in-memory capture cap does not bound live reader allocation, CPU, or total wire. No truncation into success. Preserve raw absence versus present-empty values and `null` versus `[]` when independently verified.
- **Access and disclosure:** root owner-only (0700 directory, 0600 objects), no raw frame, path, URI, result body, or server error in public output or diagnostic text. Request URIs/params and response locations are sensitive. Selected private diagnostics must independently specify redaction. A digest and selector disclose identity information and are not public by default. No source-body retention follows from this policy.
- **Replay eligibility and lazy cleanup:** record capture start and absolute expiry in the immutable **frame-diagnostic** metadata; the qualified replay reader must check expiry **before** returning raw bytes and reject access at or after 24 hours, including after restart. An untrusted clock, missing/mismatched timestamp, or rollback relative to an established watermark fails closed for qualified reads; the clock/recovery assumptions remain to be selected and tested. On startup and before private acquisition/read operations, attempt bounded cleanup of expired frames, manifests, and known temporaries/orphans, accounting separately for deletion success or failure. Failed cleanup never counts as deletion, but must not turn an expired record into a fresh one. **This is not a physical 24-hour deletion guarantee:** without a running or scheduled actor, bytes may persist indefinitely on an idle or stopped machine and remain accessible to the root custodian or backups. Name this residual privacy risk; do not label the policy `deleted within 24 hours`, claim secure erasure, or retain undeclared secondary copies. The root-level `ReadSelector` is not an expiry-aware replay API and must not be exposed as one.
- **Replay:** within the eligible window, an offline verifier must parse canonical, closed, versioned bytes; rebind request/response frames to selected method, session/generation/key, exact query and policy; recompute lengths/digests, decode one valid keyed success result, and reject mutation, unknown fields, duplicate keys, incompatible custody, missing frames, and stale records. After expiry or deletion, exact-frame re-verification is unavailable through this optional reader. The separate canonical query/result artifact may remain replayable under its own privacy/availability policy; no occurrence may be newly admitted from a digest-only substitute when its required result payload is unavailable. Grouping replay from an already admitted immutable occurrence ledger follows its separately frozen input/replay contract and must not claim optional exact-frame verification.

## Narrow private replay-window reader now available

`internal/adr0011retention.ReadFrame` uses the pinned private `publication.Root` to read one canonical, closed `v0` manifest and its exact length/digest-matching frame. It rejects expired reads **before** opening the frame and rechecks time after reading; it distinguishes clock rollback during a read, changed bytes, noncanonical/duplicate/unknown metadata, and the exact 24-hour boundary. Tests use a controllable test clock; the exported reader uses the process clock. This is a private **single-frame eligibility probe**, not a two-frame method receipt or qualified retention system: the test fixture writes local files, no production writer/manifest inventory/cleanup is wired, no independent clock trust or persisted rollback watermark exists, and a root custodian can bypass it with direct disk access. Its `v0` is not the ADR 0011 method-receipt family version. Nothing it returns admits an occurrence.

## Narrow deletion primitive now available

`internal/publication.Root.RemovePrivateFile` removes one private regular selector relative to the pinned root, then syncs its parent directory. It reports namespace removal separately from directory sync and refuses symlinks, unsafe selectors, non-private files, non-private parent directories, directories, and closed roots. It uses the same pinned-parent/final-component lock identity as `PublishBoundFile` without creating missing directories during removal. Focused tests cover exact removal, no-repeat, path replacement, and unsafe/outside targets. **This is not an expiry-aware reader, lazy cleanup implementation, deadline guarantee, or secure erasure:** it has no inventory, clock, scheduling, crash recovery, orphan/temp-file accounting, hard-link/backup accounting, deletion log, or integration with method capture; a late sync failure reports removal with durability unconfirmed. No product may claim physical deletion by 24 hours on the basis of this primitive or the selected lazy-cleanup policy.

## Private canonical-candidate review carrier (not a production receipt)

`internal/adr0011methodresult.PublishPrivateCandidate` checks the bounded canonical query/result **candidate** before no-replace publication under a caller-supplied owner-only `publication.Root`. `ReadPrivateCandidate` checks the published file's private single-link metadata, replays its canonical bytes, and compares the exact ID, session/generation/key, method, query URI/position, and parameter digest to a separately supplied expectation. The caller owns the private root and selector and must explicitly complete qualification review and invoke `RemovePrivateFile`; no automatic expiry, physical deletion deadline, retained-copy inventory, or secure erasure follows. A privileged same-process producer or root custodian can still forge or replace a self-consistent candidate; the readback is not producer authentication. These candidate APIs do not issue a production method receipt, terminal-member ledger, references target identity, occurrence, or Leiden input. Production owner/root selection, end-to-end managed publication, and independent source/version custody remain unfrozen.

## Independent gates

Selecting an optional diagnostic replay window and lazy cleanup does **not** select a canonical method-transaction codec, manager publisher, cleanup inventory, expiry-aware offline verifier, revision/document custody, references query-target identity, denominator/member ledger, occurrence admission, grouping-input policy, held-out Leiden qualification, or CLI/MCP enablement. The mandatory canonical query/result artifact has a separate privacy/replay contract and must not inherit a frame-expiry gate by accident. If optional frame diagnostics are later enabled, freeze their numeric limits and replay/cleanup policy, then falsify default-off, failure withholding, read rejection at the 24-hour boundary, restart/idle cleanup limitations, cleanup failure isolation, resource exhaustion, and exact in-window replay before permitting **diagnostic-frame** production publication. These gates do not delay the separate canonical query/result path. Existing package-private test hooks and test-owned `VERIFIED` readback remain observations only.
